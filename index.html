Cultural Perspective

Estar: a deeper sense

Indigenous thought & language

In many Indigenous traditions of the Americas, being is not a fixed identity — it is a living relationship with the land. You do not simply come from a place; you are in it, belonging to it, part of it.

The Argentine philosopher Rodolfo Kusch argued that the Western concept of ser — a stable, defined identity — misses something essential about how Indigenous peoples of Latin America understand existence. For them, estar — to be here, in this place, at this moment — comes first.

"El hombre americano no es, sino que está."
— Rodolfo Kusch, América profunda (1962)

The American man does not simply be — he is here. Presence, not definition.

SER — Origen / Origin

Ser de… — Where you're from

Use ser to express origin. Origin is fixed — it doesn't change.

ser origin
Ella es del Perú.
Ella es del Perú. RevealShe is from Peru.
Tú eres de Lima.
Tú eres de Lima. RevealYou are from Lima.
Nosotras somos de Miraflores.
Nosotras somos de Miraflores. RevealWe are from Miraflores.

Origin moves from country → city → neighborhood — ser stays the same at every level.

ESTAR — Ubicación / Location

Estar en… — Where you are

Use estar to express location. Location can change.

estar location
Él está en el Perú.
Él está en el Perú. RevealHe is in Peru.
Ustedes están en Lima.
Ustedes están en Lima. RevealYou are in Lima.
Nosotras estamos en Miraflores.
Nosotras estamos en Miraflores. RevealWe are in Miraflores.

Same places — but now as locations, not origins. You are there; you might leave tomorrow.
